A make.fun entry for **The Long Roast: Lawmakers and the File They
Cannot Convert**: one satirical sentence, at most 320 characters,
roasting lawmakers who would regulate AI, crypto or the internet but
cannot convert a file to PDF.

## The line

> They wrote the rules for crypto, AI and the whole internet into a 400-page bill, then sent it to the clerk as a phone photo of a laptop screen because no one in the building could turn a Word file into a PDF.

208 characters, one sentence. The setup grants the lawmakers their
full authority — *they wrote the rules for crypto, AI and the whole
internet into a 400-page bill* — and the turn undercuts it with a
single specific image: the bill itself reaches the clerk not as a
document but as *a phone photo of a laptop screen*. The punch lands
on the last words, *could turn a Word file into a PDF*, the exact
incompetence the bounty names. The joke stays aimed at the lawmakers
and the rules they write — power and competence, not random abuse —
and it picks a concrete, unexpected detail over the worn
"old people can't use computers" cliche: a photo of a screen is
something anyone who has worked in an office has actually received.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3, standard library only) writes the line to
`/output/entry.txt`, exactly as written — no trailing newline, no
extra words. It first checks its own output against the gate: 1–320
characters, not only whitespace, a single line, exactly one sentence.
